# Opposite-edge torus

Label the square A, B, C, D clockwise from the upper left. The arrows pair points at equal distances along two directed edges. Orange identifies A→B with D→C; cyan identifies B→C with A→D. Reading around the boundary clockwise gives a b a⁻¹ b⁻¹.

1. Roll the square until the orange edges meet. Their pointwise identification makes a cylinder with a longitudinal orange seam. A and D occupy one end of the seam, and B and C occupy the other. The two cyan circles are the cylinder’s open rims.
2. Bend the cylinder into a ring. Glue points on the rims in the same cylinder angular coordinate: the same rim marker on each end arrives at the same surface point. The two corner pairs now merge.
3. On the finished torus, orange runs around the central hole and cyan around the tube. These loops meet once. Four 90° corners supply a complete 360° neighborhood. The identifications give one vertex, two edges and one face, so the Euler characteristic is zero. The surface is closed and orientable.
