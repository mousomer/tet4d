# Opposite-edge Klein bottle

Label the square A, B, C, D clockwise from the upper left. Orange pairs A→B with D→C. Cyan pairs B→C with D→A. Reading around the square gives the boundary word a b a⁻¹ b.

1. Roll the square until the orange edges meet to make a cylinder. A and D now share a point on the left rim. B and C share a point on the right rim.
2. Match the cyan rims with their angular order reversed. The arrows run opposite ways around the cylinder when viewed from the same end. Each dot meets its matching dot, and each diamond meets its matching diamond. This gluing merges the two corner pairs.
3. The four right angles form one smooth point. The closed quotient has one vertex, two edges and one face, giving Euler characteristic zero. Reversing the second pairing gives an orientation-reversing loop and hence a nonorientable surface.

The final model uses a figure-eight immersion. Orange and cyan trace the identified square edges. The white ring marks two distinct points with the same projected position. The viewer’s **Separate crossing sheets** slider separates their projections while the identified edge points stay together.
