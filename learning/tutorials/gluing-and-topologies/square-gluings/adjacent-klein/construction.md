# Adjacent-edge Klein bottle

Label the square A, B, C, D clockwise from the upper left. Match arrow tails to tails and arrowheads to arrowheads. Orange pairs A→B with D→A. Cyan pairs B→C with C→D. Reading clockwise gives the boundary word a b b a, cyclically equivalent to a a b b.

1. Twist the sheet and join the orange edges. Their endpoints identify A with D and B with A. The intermediate surface is a Möbius band. A, B and D now occupy one point on its boundary, while C is still separate.
2. Close the cyan seam. Its endpoint identifications bring C to the shared point. Each of the four original corners supplies a 90° sector, giving one smooth 360° neighborhood.
3. Inspect the finished surface. Both seams are closed loops based at that shared point. The quotient has one vertex, two edges and one face, giving Euler characteristic zero. The orientation-reversing loop and closed surface identify the Klein bottle.

The prototype uses a figure-eight immersion. In the default projection, two distinct points on the Klein bottle coincide at the white ring. The **Separate crossing sheets** control moves their projections apart. Orange and cyan continue to trace the original square edges. Rotation, surface palette, lighting, opacity, seam visibility and the shared-vertex label can be adjusted in the viewer.
