Opposite-edge real projective plane (RP²)

Label the square A, B, C, D clockwise from the upper left. Orange pairs A→B with C→D. Cyan pairs B→C with D→A. Each pair reverses the direction along opposite sides of the square. Reading the arrows along the clockwise boundary gives the word a b a b.

  1. Give the sheet a half-twist and glue the orange edges, matching arrow tails and heads. This makes a Möbius band. A meets C, and B meets D. The two cyan edges together make its single boundary loop.
  2. Glue the cyan edges with their arrows aligned. B meets D and C meets A, so the two corner classes stay distinct as the boundary closes. At each class, two 90° sectors join to form a 180° cone point.
  3. The closed quotient has two vertices, two edges and one face. Its Euler characteristic is 2 − 2 + 1 = 1. The half-twist gives an orientation-reversing loop. The result is the real projective plane.

The final model uses a Boy’s surface immersion modified locally at both tips. In the reset view, A+C is the green tip at upper left and B+D is the magenta tip at right. The orange and cyan seam curves each connect those two tips. The final camera looks into the three apparent openings between the translucent lobes. At the central crossing, three distinct points of the surface project to the same location. The animation holds this view with both seams visible and cone labels off. In the viewer, Separate crossing sheets moves the sheets apart while the seams stay joined.

In the viewer, Replay starts from the square. Pause or scrub to inspect either seam and drag to rotate at any stage. The second seam closes at 16 seconds. The camera then turns slightly and holds the finished surface with both seams visible and labels off.
